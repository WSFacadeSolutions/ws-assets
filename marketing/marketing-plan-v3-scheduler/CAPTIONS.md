# Captions — September and October 2026 runway

## Peakhurst interior

Peakhurst, NSW

A quieter view of the work.

Inside, the decorative finish follows the curved geometry rather than flattening it.
Light moves differently across the stair, fireplace and hand-worked surface, while
the rooms continue to read as one interior.

Our scope included substrate, render, decorative finishes, paint and caulking.

## Cecil Hills elevation

Cecil Hills, NSW

One composition, held by proportion.

Acrylic render, mouldings around the openings and carefully sealed joints give the
facade its depth without competing with the original architecture.

The broad view only works when the small transitions do.

## Cabramatta contrast

Cabramatta, NSW

Light, shadow and a restrained palette do most of the work here.

Broad rendered planes are balanced by dark framing and deep-set openings, keeping
the elevation clear from both the street and the entry.

## Drummoyne threshold

Drummoyne, NSW

The facade does not stop at the front elevation.

At the rear, render, paint and sealed junctions carry the finish through the threshold
between outdoor room, pool deck and interior. Continuity is the detail.

## Bossley Park lines

Bossley Park, NSW

Edges first.

Crisp parapets, deep reveals and a limited palette give the elevation its rhythm. New
substrate, render and sealed transitions were delivered as one exterior package.

The result feels simple because the junctions are resolved.

## The Crescent outdoor room

The Crescent, Vaucluse, NSW

An outdoor room shaped by one continuous curve.

Render, decorative finishes, paint and caulking sit quietly behind the architecture,
allowing shadow, stone and planting to lead the space.

## Paddington heritage elevation

Paddington, NSW

A heritage elevation should not look newly invented. It should look cared for.

The restraint is in maintaining the rhythm between arches, mouldings, balcony
ironwork and painted surfaces, so the house keeps its own character.

## Dover Heights geometry

Dover Heights, NSW

The geometry is most visible from below.

Repainting and resealing protect the corners and exposed junctions that define this
facade, without changing the building's original language.

## Peakhurst finish detail

Peakhurst, NSW

Hand-worked surfaces change with the distance.

Up close, the variation is deliberate: a finish that catches light without becoming
pattern. From across the room, it settles back into the architecture.

## St Ives angle

St Ives, NSW

The success of a light facade is in its control: straight reveals, measured mouldings
and clean junctions.

New substrate, render, paint and caulking were carried as one coordinated exterior.

## Baulkham Hills junction

Baulkham Hills, NSW

One quiet junction: rendered wall, dark window frame and sandstone retaining wall.

The materials meet without asking for attention. That restraint is the result.
