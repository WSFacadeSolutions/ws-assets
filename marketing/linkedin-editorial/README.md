## 1 — The finishing package is a coordination problem

- Date: Thursday 3 September 2026, 8:30 am Sydney
- Asset: `assets/01-commodore-managed-package.jpg`

The best finishing packages are not managed as a collection of isolated trades.

At The Old Commodore Hotel in McMahons Point, the interior scope moved between
Venetian plaster walls, concrete-finish structural columns and the junctions
around the bar, stair and dining spaces.

Each finish had its own application requirements. The harder problem was making
them read as one interior across long sightlines, busy circulation and constant
contact.

That is why coordination matters as much as application. Sequencing, substrate
readiness, protection and handover all affect the final surface — even when they
are no longer visible.

WS delivered the interior and exterior finishing package as one managed scope.

## 2 — The finish starts before the finish

- Date: Thursday 17 September 2026, 8:30 am Sydney
- Asset: `assets/02-finish-starts-before-finish.jpg`

The finish starts before the finish.

A decorative surface can only be as controlled as the substrate, junctions and
preparation beneath it. By the time the final coat is being applied, many of the
decisions that determine the result have already been made.

We look at the work in sequence:

• what the substrate needs
• how adjacent materials will meet
• where movement or moisture must be managed
• how the area will be protected between trades
• what the finish needs to achieve under real light

This is not extra process around the craft. It is part of the craft.

Precision in the visible layer begins with control in every layer before it.

## 3 — From close detail to architectural context

- Date: Thursday 15 October 2026, 8:30 am Sydney
- Asset: `assets/03-the-crescent-context.jpg`

Close-up finish photographs are useful, but distance tells a different truth.

Seen from the water's edge, The Crescent in Vaucluse becomes a composition of
rendered planes, dark screens, deep overhangs, sandstone and mature planting.
The facade has to give the architecture definition without separating the home
from its setting.

WS delivered render, decorative finishes, paint and caulking across the project.
Those individual scopes disappear in the completed view — which is exactly the
point. A coordinated finishing package should support the architecture, not ask
to be noticed as a collection of trades.

## 4 — Different hands, one managed standard

- Date: Thursday 29 October 2026, 8:30 am Sydney
- Asset: `assets/04-one-shared-standard.jpg`

Consistency on site does not come from asking every person to work in exactly
the same way. It comes from making the required standard clear, checking the
conditions early and keeping decisions close to the work.

At WS, Heron de Freitas Santiago and Thomas Wilhelm lead from both sides of that
responsibility: engineering, project management and business operations
alongside rendering, site logistics and workforce performance.

The objective is straightforward — different specialists, one managed standard.

That means issues are raised before they become defects, resources are treated
with care, and the finished surface is judged as part of the whole project.

Many trades. One contract. One shared responsibility for the result.
